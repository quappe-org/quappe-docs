# Decision: accessibility modes named by function, not diagnosis

**Status:** decided. Do not rename these back.

## The request and the split

The ask was for themes like "ADHS / neurodivergent / conservative / unicorn".
We deliberately split that into two kinds of thing:

- **Aesthetic wishes → themes.** *unicorn* kept. *conservative* became
  **classic** (muted/editorial) — a political label on a neutral-discourse
  platform sends the wrong signal.
- **Access needs → functional modes.** *ADHS / neurodivergent* became **calm**.

## Why not name a mode after a diagnosis

Naming a mode "ADHS" reduces people to a diagnosis — like calling a wheelchair
ramp a "disabled mode". **Calm** (reduced visual noise) helps anyone distracted
by a busy UI: ADHD, autism, migraine, or just a noisy room. That is inclusive,
not labelling — and it fits the humanist mission. The functional name is *why*
the mode exists and who it serves, without singling anyone out.

## The modes

`invert` · `calm` · `high contrast` · `reduce motion` — orthogonal toggles that
combine with any aesthetic theme. `reduce motion` also respects the OS
`prefers-reduced-motion` automatically.
