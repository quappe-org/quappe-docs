# The idea

## The problem

Language isn't precise — it always needs definition. Formalising positions and
finding what people actually share is hard. Social media as we know it
(Instagram, TikTok, …) makes it worse: it manufactures bubbles, fronts, and
hate rather than understanding.

## The target

Quappe is an **argumentation graph** for reaching *defined, coexisting*
positions on a topic — a space that shows **what leads someone to an opinion**.

A position rests on beliefs and, at best, on real facts (properly-set-up
peer-reviewed studies) or valid authorities acting with integrity. Theses can
depend on and relate to one another, so dependencies between positions are
visible.

Truth — even with facts — is relative to subjects and circumstances. So the
goal is not a single winner but:

- **find as much consent as possible** on a topic, and
- **show disagreement with respect.**

Other positions are OK. But *qualify yourself*: an opinion without a proper line
of argument simply weighs less in the discourse. And a topic usually has
**several** commonalities, not one — Quappe looks for those.

## What it is not

- Not an exposure of the vulnerable.
- Not a fighting place (argument happens *inside* arguments, not against people).
- Not a comment section.

## The rules

1. The integrity of a being is untouchable. Always.
2. Separate the person from the position. Always.
3. Good faith. Assume it. Earn it.
4. Minorities deserve a voice and a safe space, but the majority is the majority.
5. Trolls get marked kindly first, firmly later. Repeated disturbance leads to
   ignore or exclusion.
6. Qualify yourself. Bring reasoning, bring evidence, bring nuance.

## The system must bend toward the good

A deliberate design stance: the mechanics should structurally favour humanism
and diversity — foster goodwill and commonality. Disruptors may stay *visible*,
but they must not *win*; and even if a majority turns "wrong", a good minority
position must be able to **germinate**. Vote direction is treated neutrally
(support == reject for visibility) and a decaying germination boost gives fresh
positions a fair window to grow. We accept the logical imperfections of this —
the nature of the system should nudge toward the good.
